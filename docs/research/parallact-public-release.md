# ParallACT public release: targeted evidence

Checked 9 November 2026. This assesses the proposed 12 ready feature records and calculated labels, not a dump of the research cache. It supplements and corrects the weight given to the website footer in [the earlier assessment](parallact-reuse-terms.md).

## Decision

**Conditional GO for the curated twelve-pair task-and-label bundle under the packaging terms below.** Credit alone is insufficient, but there is a reasonable existing-license basis for release. No extra permission letter is inherently required before relying on that basis. This recommendation relies on interpreting the site's CC BY-SA notice to cover its explorer content, not on a new author confirmation or a guarantee of every contributor's rights.

| Material | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| IDs, provenance, hashes and calculated labels | GO. These are already public in the recovery case ledger. |
| The 24 matched task statements | Conditional GO with CC presentation terms, source-specific upstream terms and notices preserved. |
| Agent patches, full trajectories and source archives | Outside this release assessment. Their redistribution is not cleared here. |

Opus 5.5 at high effort independently reviewed the source facts and accepted this release recommendation subject to the specific packaging terms. The earlier blanket local-only policy was too restrictive for this limited bundle. No raw task text has been published as part of this investigation.

## Decision evidence

**There is a credible case for releasing the 12 task pairs with credit and CC BY-SA 4.0 compliance.** The [project website](https://lingyunxiao18.github.io/ParallACT/) states, "This website is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License." It identifies the project authors and invites readers to browse all instances, read both requirements and inspect runs in its [dataset explorer](https://lingyunxiao18.github.io/ParallACT/explorer.html). The explorer's [JavaScript](https://lingyunxiao18.github.io/ParallACT/assets/js/util.js) fetches run JSON from the same website. A reasonable plain-language reading is that the grant covers website content, including the task statements presented by the explorer and possibly its backing JSON, to the extent the licensor has rights. The footer's template origin does not negate that grant. Treating it as necessarily too weak to rely on was overly cautious.

**The grant's exact reach and rights chain are not author-confirmed.** The footer says "this website," not explicitly "all dataset files, including those in the separate GitHub repository." The explorer has no footer of its own. The website does not identify the individual or entity making the grant beyond its project attribution; the authors' authority over every embedded third-party contribution has not been established. A website license is not affirmative proof that all third-party task text is sublicensed. There is no identified explicit exclusion of the explorer data, and no statement found that release is forbidden. These facts support a reasonable interpretation, not a verified blanket dataset grant. They do not establish that written permission is mandatory.

The [dataset repository README at `c212deb174fbc8980212cc1bde5b3f047941f332`](https://github.com/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/blob/c212deb174fbc8980212cc1bde5b3f047941f332/README.md) describes the data and asks for citation but does not state a dataset license. Its [complete recursive tree](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/git/trees/c212deb174fbc8980212cc1bde5b3f047941f332?recursive=1) has 925 entries and no license, licence, copyright, notice or terms filename; the [license API](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/license) returns 404. That is evidence of no repository-wide license found, not a ban. The current [branch listing](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/branches) has only `main` at that pin; seven [commits](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/commits?per_page=100), no [tags](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/tags) or [releases](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/releases), no [issues](https://api.github.com/repos/rm206/ParallACT_data_and_code/issues?state=all&per_page=100), and disabled discussions were returned. Checks of the likely website repositories returned 404; the website's Pages source and history are not established. Searches of the [Hugging Face dataset API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?search=ParallACT) and [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/api/records/?q=ParallACT&size=5) found no matching card or archive. The [paper's arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00314v1) links an article distribution license, not an express dataset grant.

## What attribution and the license actually do

[Creative Commons' own application guidance](https://creativecommons.org/cc-license-your-work/) expressly allows a website footer as a licensing notice. A separate LICENSE file, a bespoke declaration or a new permission email is not inherently required when the relevant work is already licensed. The notice still needs an identifiable scope. Its template origin does not cancel its wording.

The [BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en) grants copying and public sharing of licensed material. Credit is one condition, alongside retaining supplied notices, linking the license, marking changes and applying an allowed ShareAlike license to adaptations. The grant covers rights the licensor can grant; it does not automatically relicense embedded third-party material. The [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#does-a-creative-commons-license-give-me-all-the-rights-i-need-to-use-the-work) explains that distinction. Collections can preserve separate component licenses; placing independent works together does not automatically replace those terms with one blanket license.

[GitHub's licensing guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository) distinguishes public access and platform forking from a general reuse license. [Terms of Service D.6](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service#6-contributions-under-repository-license) also says contributions to a repository containing a license notice are licensed under those terms. Therefore individual issue contributions are not automatically unlicensed. This is background, not an additional release gate. The operative upstream notice is SWE-bench Pro's maintainer statement about its task content. An original-issue history investigation is not required by this release recommendation. The [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/m10.pdf) likewise distinguishes licenses and applicable exceptions from simply being able to access a work.

The operative reason to publish would be an applicable license or other legal basis, followed by compliance with its terms. Public availability and a paper citation alone are not that basis. A reasonable reading of the site's existing grant is a possible basis; it must not be described as newly confirmed author permission for every asset.

## What the 12 records contain

The private `data/private/parallact/recovery/features.json` has 12 records with IDs, repository, base commit and actual `task_a` and `task_b` text. `targets.json` contains the corresponding locally calculated merge labels and completion status. The [public case ledger](../../data/parallact-recovery/cases.json) records source URLs, hashes and association checks. Across all 12, a fresh bounded fetch confirmed that both task fields exactly equal the corresponding pinned GitHub seed fields and match the website run prompts after removing the prompt prefix and normalizing outer whitespace. All 24 responses returned 200 and matched recorded SHA-256 hashes. Six records are under the repository's `seeds` path and six under `generated`; all are v1. This establishes content overlap, not an independently verified license or website source revision.

The [project page](https://lingyunxiao18.github.io/ParallACT/) says A comes from SWE-bench Pro, while B is a separately motivated counterpart; 27 benchmark instances were hand-built and 128 generated using GPT 5.6 Sol. Thus, check A's upstream provenance as well as any ParallACT rights in B. The [current SWE-bench Pro card at `2d52cb3df914a3fcf80c7f66738b3a88ae37fc50`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ScaleAI/SWE-bench_Pro/blob/2d52cb3df914a3fcf80c7f66738b3a88ae37fc50/README.md) distinguishes MIT-licensed tooling from task content, including statements, patches and tests, which it says remains subject to the respective upstream repository licenses. The older [v1 card at `307bd006a0a3807424d3c5525eaf98ca8e63aed3`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ScaleAI/SWE-bench_Pro/blob/307bd006a0a3807424d3c5525eaf98ca8e63aed3/README.md) contains no corresponding license section. The current card says its v1 configuration preserves original values. A targeted dataset-server query for one A statement failed with HTTP 500, so an exact field comparison to SWE-bench Pro and individual issue or contributor provenance remain unchecked. This affects whether we can call the A text verbatim or augmented, not the recommended source-specific notice: use "as presented in ParallACT, derived from SWE-bench Pro."

GitHub's license API at each sampled base commit identifies Teleport as Apache-2.0, Open Library as AGPL-3.0, and Proton Mail WebClients, Navidrome, NodeBB, Flipt, Vuls and Ansible as GPL-3.0. Base commits and repository links are in the [ledger](../../data/parallact-recovery/cases.json). These are repository-level detections, not proof that each statement is covered by the code license or that every issue author withheld a grant. Do not automatically apply source-code copyleft to plain requirements. Assess the actual copied material and any applicable notices separately.

## Required packaging for the limited release

1. Publish a collection of twelve paired task statements and our calculated labels, not the private workspaces. Identify each statement's website `run_url` from the [case ledger](../../data/parallact-recovery/cases.json) as the licensed presented source. Keep the pinned GitHub seed URL/hash as provenance for the matching content, without claiming its unlicensed repository supplies the grant.
2. Give the collection and our contributions a CC BY-SA 4.0 notice while preserving each component's existing terms. For `task_b`, record CC BY-SA 4.0 from the website presentation. For `task_a`, record that presentation basis **and** the upstream terms named by SWE-bench Pro: Apache-2.0 for Teleport, AGPL-3.0 for Open Library, and GPL-3.0 for the other sampled repositories. Preserve source declarations rather than relabeling all component text CC-only. This is a collection with component notices, not an invented dual-license choice.
3. Include full texts of CC BY-SA 4.0, GPL-3.0, AGPL-3.0 and Apache-2.0, with per-record links to the relevant upstream base commits and applicable supplied notices. [CC's compatible-license list](https://creativecommons.org/compatible-licenses/) permits BY-SA 4.0 adaptations to move to GPLv3 in one direction only. It does not permit converting GPL-covered text to CC BY-SA. Keeping independent components separately marked avoids pretending that all fields have one interchangeable license. The review's root-directory checks found no root NOTICE at the two Teleport bases; that is a bounded observation, not clearance for arbitrary Apache files or archives.
4. Credit Rishabh Mediratta, Lingyun Xiao, Peter Stone, Joydeep Biswas and Elias Stengel-Eskin, linking the [project](https://lingyunxiao18.github.io/ParallACT/) and [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00314). Credit SWE-bench Pro and Scale AI, linking the [pinned dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ScaleAI/SWE-bench_Pro/blob/2d52cb3df914a3fcf80c7f66738b3a88ae37fc50/README.md), and each upstream repository at its base commit. Retain provided copyright and license notices without implying endorsement.
5. State the actual changes: selected twelve benchmark pairs, matched task wording to the website copies, removed execution-prompt prefixes, normalized outer whitespace if doing so in the release, and added locally computed merge labels. Make no claim to rights beyond those supplied by the licensors. No substantive task wording was rewritten.
6. Document the hash convention. `task_sha256` hashes UTF-8 `statement.strip()`, not the raw JSON representation. Three feature records have outer whitespace. The public bundle should either normalize those strings or retain them and explain why raw-text hashes differ. Preserve private verified originals unchanged.

Metadata and labels are already published, not merely a fallback proposal. A task-and-label release would add the statements and packaging above. Patches, trajectories, source archives and logs are unnecessary for that bundle and remain outside this decision. The collection's license notice must not purport to relicense the entire CollisionGuard code repository.

## Probe limits

The 12 seed JSON and 12 compressed website runs were read without downloading a dataset or source archive. Their top-level JSON keys and HTTP response headers contained no fields named for license, copyright, rights, terms or attribution; that is not a search of all free text or other files. The mutable website run bytes were checked against ledger hashes, not an identified Pages commit. No author was contacted and no source text was redistributed. Whether any particular model output, task statement or compilation has additional rights or exceptions was not decided here.
